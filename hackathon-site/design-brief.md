# Design brief: Cville AI Explorers homepage

**Concept.** A one-page site for a local AI meetup that feels like a community bulletin board. Bold editorial type sits beside hand-drawn marks: brush-stroke underlines, sketched checkboxes, a curved arrow, sticky notes, contour lines and a dot grid. The look came from a mood board of warm, people-centered imagery. It deliberately avoids the generic "AI startup" style of purple gradients, glassy cards and glowing tech art. It uses no photos of people at all rather than stock or AI-generated faces, and every word on the page comes from the group's own content files.

**Audience and first screen.** The site is for Charlottesville-area AI enthusiasts, from active builders to curious newcomers. Its first job is logistics: when and where the next meetup is, and how to RSVP. The first screen pairs the group name and a one-line welcome with a "Next meetup" card that gives the date, title, venue, description and an RSVP button. When no future meetup is set, the card says "TBA: Check Meetup Page" and links to the group page. Mission, history and sponsor information come after that.

**Visual system.** The neutrals are near-black ink `#121418` for dark sections and text, warm cream `#F4EFE6` for the main light background, and a lighter paper `#FBF8F2` for the About section. Signal orange `#FF5A2E` is the one main accent: buttons, highlighted words, the card's offset shadow and the full-orange Join section. Orange text uses darker `#C63A12` on light backgrounds and lighter `#FF7A55` on dark ones. Blue `#1454F5` is used for brush underlines and checkmarks, teal `#1F8A95` for contour lines (`#16707A` when it's text), and mint `#8FE3D3` for sticky notes and tags. Muted grey `#C9C3B7` is for secondary text on dark backgrounds. The site uses four typefaces:
- **Barlow Condensed** (700–800, sentence case, tight leading) for the big statements: the site name, "Join us.", section headlines, dates and stat numbers.
- **Fraunces**, a soft serif (about 600–800), for headings such as "Where we meet", the mission statement and meetup titles.
- **Barlow** (400–700) for body text, nav and buttons, plus small bold uppercase, widely spaced labels like "OUR MISSION".
- **Caveat** (500–700), a handwriting face, for annotations, checklists and sticky notes.

All four are self-hosted. Close substitutes are any heavy condensed sans (Oswald, Archivo Narrow), a warm text serif (Newsreader, Source Serif), a plain sans (Work Sans, Public Sans) and a casual marker-style hand (Kalam, Patrick Hand).

**Layout.** The page is one long scroll of full-width sections that alternate dark, light and orange. In order: the hero with the meetup card, "Where we meet" with a Studio IX address card, About (mission statement, a whiteboard-style "we gather to" checklist and four activity tiles), History (a "since March 2025" headline, two stats, and linked cards for every past meetup, newest first), Join us with a "Get involved" sticky note, the sponsor section and the footer. Desktop sections use uneven two-column splits, a wide text column next to a narrower card. On tablets and phones they stack into one column, the activity and meetup grids drop from four or three columns to two and then one, and the nav shrinks to the logo and one Meetup button.

**Motion and behavior.** Motion is minimal. Nav links scroll smoothly to their section, and past-meetup cards lift slightly on hover; both are turned off when the visitor has reduced motion enabled. A small script compares the meetup's date with today's date to choose between the RSVP card and the TBA card. Without JavaScript the page shows the TBA card, which is always safe. The sponsor's "Learn more" button reveals Studio IX's details in place of a "Thank you, Studio IX!" note.

**Principles worth carrying over.** Put "when and where" before "who we are". Use hand-drawn marks as accents against a strict type system, never instead of it. Pick one loud accent and adjust its shade for each background so text stays readable. Present history as linked records of real events rather than claims. Make the page's default state the one that can't go stale. Ship it with no dependencies on other servers.
